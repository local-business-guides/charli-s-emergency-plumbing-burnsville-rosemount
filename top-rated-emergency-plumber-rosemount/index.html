Questions to ask an emergency plumber before the visit

Start with the symptom you can actually observe. A puddle below a sink, dripping near a water heater, and water appearing through a ceiling give a plumber different information. Avoid guessing at a diagnosis when you call.

Ask what details would help explain the situation. Photos may show the location of a leak or the space around a fixture, but they don't replace an inspection. Mention any earlier repairs in the same spot and anything that changed just before the trouble began.

A Rosemount homeowner's preparation checklist

You don't need to dismantle anything before a plumbing visit. Focus on making the affected area easier to inspect and keeping a useful record of what happened.

  • Write down when you first noticed the problem.
  • Note which fixtures were running at the time.
  • Photograph visible water damage from a safe location.
  • Clear household items away from the affected cabinet or fixture.
  • Keep children and pets out of the work area.
  • Have any relevant repair paperwork available.

Tell the plumber if the issue comes and goes. A leak that appears only when the dishwasher drains can be harder to explain once everything is dry. Describe that pattern instead of running equipment again just to recreate the problem.

Emergency plumbing mistakes that complicate a visit

Don't keep using a fixture that's visibly leaking or overflowing. Repeated testing can spread water and make cleanup harder. Avoid opening walls, loosening connections, or removing equipment simply to get a closer look.

Be careful around standing water near outlets, appliances, or electrical equipment. Stay out of the affected area when there's a possible electrical hazard. Let the person taking your call know about that condition right away.
